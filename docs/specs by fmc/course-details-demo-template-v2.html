What is BSc Psychology?

A simple explanation for students and parents

About 180 words

BSc Psychology is an undergraduate course that studies human behaviour, emotions, thought processes, learning, memory and social interaction. It uses scientific methods to understand why people behave differently in different situations.

Students commonly study areas such as cognitive psychology, developmental psychology, social psychology, biological psychology, research methods and statistics. Depending on the university, the course may include laboratory activities, surveys, case analysis, field visits, projects or internships.

This degree can be a foundation for careers in research, human resources, education, community support and behavioural services. However, professional roles such as clinical psychologist or counselling psychologist generally require postgraduate education, supervised training and any registration required by the relevant authority.

Is this course suitable for you?

Interest, learning preference and reality check

Student decision support

You may enjoy this course if you…

  • Are curious about thoughts, feelings and behaviour.
  • Enjoy reading, discussion, observation and research.
  • Can listen patiently and respect different viewpoints.
  • Are willing to study statistics and scientific methods.

Think carefully if you…

  • Expect the course to involve only counselling conversations.
  • Strongly dislike reading, report writing or basic statistics.
  • Expect to become a clinical psychologist immediately after graduation.
  • Do not want further study for specialised professional roles.
